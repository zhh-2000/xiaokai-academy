Digital Education Program Boosts Rural Schooling in Yunnan

A digital education program organized by Beijing Normal University has been carried out in Lancang Lahu Autonomous County, Yunnan Province. The project aims to promote digital education in remote ethnic minority areas and serves approximately 220,000 Lahu students in the region.

More than 30 teachers and students from Beijing Normal University participated in the three-day program at Lancang Ethnic Middle School. They taught interdisciplinary courses covering art, science, physical education, and technology to 14 classes with over 600 students. Thirty students attended an AI creative workshop, and the entire school participated in film appreciation and aesthetic education activities.

In addition, the team provided AI-empowered training for 117 teachers from 23 primary and secondary schools across the county. The training covered AI literacy assessment, the national smart education platform, WPS AI tools, AI classroom management, and teacher professional development.

The project is part of Beijing Normal University's rural digital education initiative launched in 2023. Lancang is the third practice site nationwide and the first in Yunnan. The team plans to develop localized digital teaching content, establish a tiered AI literacy training model, and provide ongoing support through both online and offline channels.

East China Normal University Students Extend Tech Education to Africa

Student volunteers from East China Normal University's School of Education Science have expanded their technology education practice to African countries along the Belt and Road. During the summer, they traveled to Karamay in Xinjiang, Xundian in Yunnan, and Liling in Hunan, as well as Namibia, Rwanda, Kenya, Tanzania, and Mauritius.

The project uses a standardized curriculum system that integrates local culture into the courses. For example, in Karamay, students made windmill models; in Xundian, they practiced tie-dyeing; and in Liling, they painted paper umbrellas. The approach encourages students to "learn by doing."

In Africa, the volunteers conducted interviews, classroom observations, and in-depth exchanges with local teachers and students. They discovered that many African countries have included technology education in their national education agendas, but they generally face challenges of teacher shortages and insufficient curriculum resources.

The volunteers' curriculum design experience was welcomed by local educators. The research findings will be compiled into a report to provide references for Belt and Road technology education cooperation. The project has benefited over 1,000 primary and secondary school students and teachers globally.

Source: China Daily, 2026-09-03
