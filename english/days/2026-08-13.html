Passage 1: China's New Science Education Initiative

As primary and middle school students across China return to school in September, they will spend more time on hands-on scientific projects, with students in grades four through nine required to complete at least one scientific inquiry project each semester, according to a guideline issued by the Ministry of Education. The guideline, which launches the "learning by doing" initiative and takes effect in the fall semester, aims to address the overemphasis on knowledge transmission and the relative neglect of competency development in science education. This new approach encourages students to explore, think, and practice through actual scientific inquiry activities, fostering their scientific spirit and innovative thinking.

Passage 2: China's Natural Resource Management

China is accelerating the construction of a "one map" platform for natural resources, according to the Ministry of Natural Resources. This initiative aims to create a comprehensive digital system that integrates various natural resource data, including land, minerals, forests, and water resources. The platform will enable better monitoring, management, and protection of natural resources across the country. By using advanced technologies such as satellite remote sensing and big data analysis, the system will provide real-time updates on resource conditions, helping decision-makers make more informed choices about sustainable development and environmental protection.
