China Achieves First Two-Way Laser Communication Between Earth and Moon

On August 29, 2026, China announced a major breakthrough in space technology: the country had successfully established a two-way laser communication link over a distance of more than 400,000 kilometers between Earth and the moon. This achievement marks China's first two-way high-speed laser communication in cislunar space, transitioning from near-Earth orbit to the Earth-moon realm.

The breakthrough was reported by the Science and Technology Daily, citing the Technology and Engineering Center for Space Utilization under the Chinese Academy of Sciences. The new laser communication system overcame technical bottlenecks including long-distance interference, dynamic tracking, and signal attenuation in lunar-Earth communication, achieving stable and efficient two-way data transmission across the Earth-moon orbital distance.

Unlike traditional radio communication, the laser system offers significantly higher bandwidth and faster transmission speeds. For example, transmitting an 8K-resolution photo of the lunar surface previously took about five minutes using radio signals, but the new laser system can accomplish the same task in just twelve seconds. This dramatic improvement will be crucial for China's future crewed lunar landings, the construction of lunar research stations, and deep-space exploration missions.

The achievement drew comparisons to the ancient Chinese poem "Viewing the Sea" by Cao Cao, written in the year 207 AD, which contains the famous lines: "The sun and moon's journey seems to emerge from it; the Milky Way's brilliance seems to arise from within it." Nearly 1,800 years later, Chinese scientists have turned this poetic imagination into technological reality, connecting the Earth and the moon with a beam of light.

Source: China Daily, Science and Technology Daily, 2026-08-29
