PALLAS-1 Y1 Rocket Succeeds in Maiden Flight

On September 1, 2026, at 10:00 a.m., the PALLAS-1 Y1 liquid carrier rocket blasted off from the Dongfeng commercial space innovation pilot zone in northwest China and successfully reached its designated orbit. This marked a new milestone in China's commercial space development.

The PALLAS-1 was independently developed by Galactic Energy, a Chinese private rocket company. It is a two-stage configuration rocket with a total length of 52 meters. Its core stage diameter is 3.35 meters, with a lift-off mass of approximately 283 tonnes and a lift-off thrust of 350 tonnes. The rocket is designed to be reused at least 25 times, making it a major expansion of the company's launch capabilities from small and medium-sized solid rockets into the reusable liquid-fueled rocket sector.

The main propulsion system uses the self-developed CQ-50 pintle-type reusable liquid oxygen/kerosene engines, whose accumulated hot-fire test duration exceeds 23,000 seconds. The rocket's first stage adopts a parallel configuration of seven CQ-50 engines, providing powerful and reliable thrust during flight.

The maiden flight has obtained full-vehicle flight data and verified the rocket's overall capabilities. This was the first flight mission of the PALLAS-1 carrier rocket. Galactic Energy next aims to carry out the first-stage vertical landing and recovery, further advancing China's reusable rocket technology.

On the afternoon of September 1, 2026, more emergency humanitarian aid supplies provided by the Chinese government departed from Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport in south China's Guangdong Province, bound for mudslide-hit Nepal. The supplies were loaded onto a Chinese Air Force Y-20 large transport aircraft and flown to Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu.

This was the second batch of aid supplies from China to Nepal, with a total weight of approximately 5.8 tonnes. The supplies included water purification equipment, lighting equipment, genetic testing equipment (DNA testing instruments and reagents), and drones — all urgently needed items for the disaster relief effort. These supplies were expected to arrive in Kathmandu on the evening of September 1.

Five experts from Shenzhen accompanied the supplies to the disaster area in Nepal. In addition, Chinese DNA identification experts were to travel to the frontline of the disaster zone to assist Nepal in carrying out their work. To ensure the aid supplies reached the disaster area as quickly as possible, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Ministry of Commerce, the General Administration of Customs, and the China International Development Cooperation Agency coordinated efforts to urgently secure supplies, transport them overnight, and complete loading procedures at Shenzhen airport.

Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun stated at a regular press conference that providing emergency assistance to Nepal's disaster-hit areas and carrying out international rescue cooperation is an important political decision made by China, reflecting its friendly sentiments toward Nepal. "China will stand firmly with Nepal, hand in hand to overcome the difficulties, and interpret the traditional friendship between neighbors with concrete actions," Guo said.

Source: Xinhua, People's Daily, The Paper, 2026-09-01
