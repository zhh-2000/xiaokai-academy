A Green Promise Across Oceans

On August 23, 2026, an American man named Ronald Saccorsky arrived at Ordos Ejin Horo Airport in Inner Mongolia, China. He had traveled for more than thirty hours. When he walked out of the terminal, a Chinese woman named Yin Yuzhen was waiting for him. They hugged each other and cried. It had been more than twenty years since they last met.

The story began in 1999. Ronald was an English teacher in China through a teacher exchange program. He learned about Yin Yuzhen, a woman who had spent her life planting trees in the Mu Us Desert, one of the largest deserts in China. Ronald raised 5,000 US dollars and sent it to Yin Yuzhen to help her buy tree seedlings. With this money, Yin and her husband planted more than 50,000 trees in the desert.

In 2000, Ronald visited Yin Yuzhen in the Mu Us Desert. They planted trees together and took photos. After that visit, they lost contact. For more than twenty years, the trees grew tall and strong, but the two friends could not find each other.

In May 2026, Yin Yuzhen posted a video online, looking for her American friend. She called him "Saikousi" (赛考斯), the Chinese name she had given him. Millions of people watched the video and helped spread the word. Finally, with the help of internet users, Ronald was found. On August 23, 2026, they met again in Ordos. The green promise made decades ago was finally fulfilled.

Yin Yuzhen is now a national labor hero. She has planted millions of trees and turned parts of the desert into green land. Ronald said he was deeply moved by her spirit and determination. "Don't thank me," he said, "thank her. She is the real hero."

Four Typhoons Dance Together

In late August 2026, an extremely rare weather phenomenon occurred over the Northwest Pacific and South China Sea: four typhoons were active at the same time. They were Shandel (the 18th), Zitan (the 19th), Jianlavi (the 20th), and Aisani (the 21st). This was called "four typhoons dancing together" (四台共舞).

Typhoon Shandel reached super typhoon strength on August 24. It was accelerating toward China and was expected to enter the East China Sea on August 26. It might make landfall along the coast of Zhejiang and Fujian provinces on August 27. The central meteorological station issued a blue typhoon warning.

Typhoon Zitan was hovering over the Beibu Gulf, bringing heavy rain to Guangxi, Guangdong, and Hainan. In Nanning, the capital of Guangxi, the Yong River rose significantly, flooding riverside parks and walkways. Rescue workers in Guangxi helped transfer trapped residents to safe areas.

Typhoon Jianlavi had already affected Fujian, Zhejiang, and Taiwan, and was expected to weaken over the Taiwan Strait on August 24. Typhoon Aisani, a tropical storm, was just formed on the morning of August 24.

The chief forecaster of the Central Meteorological Station explained that so far in 2026, 20 typhoons had formed in the Northwest Pacific and South China Sea, which was 8 more than the historical average for the same period. This was mainly related to the current El Nino conditions. Under El Nino, typhoon activity in the Western Pacific becomes more active.

Local governments in Hainan, Guangxi, and Guangdong organized fishing boats to return to ports and fishermen to come ashore. Scenic spots and construction sites in affected areas were closed. The National Flood Control Headquarters maintained a level-four emergency response for these three provinces.
