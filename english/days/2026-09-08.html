Rescue Teams Intensify Deep Searches in Gyirong

Forty-three people are confirmed dead and 519 remain missing in Gyirong county, Xizang autonomous region, after a devastating mudslide swept through the area on August 26, as search and rescue operations continued, local authorities said on September 7, 2026.

As of Saturday evening, more than 2,500 rescuers had been deployed, along with over 500 vehicles and more than 9,000 sets of rescue equipment. Drones had made over 1,000 flights, while 5.43 metric tons of supplies and equipment had been air-dropped.

Since road access to the core area was restored on Wednesday, six specialized groups and seven rescue teams have been carrying out detailed searches. Rescuers have been clearing debris layer by layer and searching each section thoroughly, with some areas already cleared down to the original foundations.

The State Council Information Office and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs organized a visit to the disaster area for 17 journalists from nine overseas media outlets, including those from the United States, Britain, Russia, India and Singapore. Bruno Falci, a Brazilian journalist, said it was "really impressive how fast China acts," citing the rapid mobilization of rescuers and reopening of roads.

A high-altitude ice-rock collapse on the Nepalese side of the Himalayas triggered the disaster. The resulting debris flow traveled about 22 kilometers downstream in only six to seven minutes, causing severe damage. Su Pengcheng, a researcher at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, said climate change is increasing the risks of disasters in the Himalayas, calling for early glacier-risk assessment and stronger cross-border cooperation.

This means adding approximately 1 billion kilowatts of new renewable energy capacity in the next five years.

During the 14th Five-Year Plan period, China built the world's largest and fastest-growing renewable energy system. The new plan aims to make renewable power generation reach about 6 trillion kilowatt-hours annually by 2030.

The State Grid Corporation of China unveiled plans to lift the share of renewable power generation across its operating footprint to over 30 percent by 2030, committing to meet all incremental electricity demand with clean energy. The comprehensive decarbonization blueprint includes integrating large-scale renewable energy into the power system.

China's photovoltaic power capacity has already overtaken coal-fired power for the first time. Energy plans for the 15th Five-Year Plan period set out specific measures to better integrate renewable energy into the power system, making PV power more predictable and dispatchable. The country aims to achieve a green and low-carbon power supply pattern by 2030.

Experts note that this transition represents a historic shift in China's energy structure. Non-fossil energy power generation is expected to reach 50 percent by 2030, marking a crucial step toward carbon neutrality. The plan also emphasizes grid modernization, energy storage development, and smart power systems to handle the intermittency of renewable sources.
