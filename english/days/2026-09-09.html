China Dispatches Fourth Batch of Emergency Relief Supplies to Nepal

China dispatched its fourth batch of emergency relief supplies to Nepal on the morning of September 8, 2026, as the Himalayan nation continued to grapple with the devastating aftermath of catastrophic floods and mudslides that have claimed over 1,357 lives.

Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning announced at a regular press briefing that the supplies — delivered by a Chinese Air Force Y-20 transport aircraft to Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu — include rescue hovercraft, stainless steel permanent magnet flood pumps, backup batteries, mortuary cabinets, and solar lamps, tailored to Nepal's most urgent needs.

Chinese Ambassador to Nepal Zhang Maoming and Nepal's Minister of Science, Technology and Innovation Mahabir Pun attended the handover ceremony at the airport. This latest delivery follows three earlier batches sent on August 30, September 1, and September 3.

Mao also highlighted broader civil-society contributions: the China Foundation for Rural Development has established temporary shelters in disaster-hit areas of Nepal, providing daily necessities and basic medical services. The Nepal Chinese Enterprises Association recently handed over its first batch of disaster relief funds to the Nepali side.

"In the face of major disasters, China will continue to stand together with Nepal and join hands to overcome difficulties," Mao said. The death toll from the Nepal mudslide disaster has risen to 1,357 as of September 8, according to Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority.

China's Foreign Trade Surges 17.6% in First Eight Months of 2026

China's foreign trade in goods rose 17.6 percent year-on-year in the first eight months of 2026 to 34.78 trillion yuan (about $5.13 trillion), according to data released by the General Administration of Customs on September 8, 2026. The growth rate accelerated by 0.3 percentage points compared to the January-July period.

Exports climbed 14.6 percent to 20.17 trillion yuan, while imports surged 22 percent to 14.61 trillion yuan. The import growth rate exceeded the export growth rate by 8 percentage points, underscoring China's expanding role as a major consumer market. In August alone, imports grew by an impressive 24.5 percent year-on-year.

The ASEAN remained China's largest trading partner, with bilateral trade reaching 4.82 trillion yuan, up 17.2 percent. The European Union ranked second with trade totaling 4.46 trillion yuan, a 16.7 percent increase, followed by the United States at 3.88 trillion yuan, up 17.4 percent.

Trade with Belt and Road countries grew 19.4 percent to 16.98 trillion yuan, while trade with other BRICS nations increased 18.8 percent. Mechanical and electrical products led exports, accounting for 12.15 trillion yuan, up 16.2 percent. Automobile exports reached 6.52 million units, maintaining China's position as the world's top auto exporter.

The 2026 China International Fair for Trade in Services (CIFTIS) is set to open in Beijing on September 9, further highlighting China's commitment to expanding trade. The fair, held at the newly renovated Shougang Park, will run through September 13 with exhibitors from over 80 countries.

Source: China Daily / Xinhua / CCTV News, 2026-09-08
